# CareerDeck content policy

**Version:** 2026-09-24
**Applies to:** comments on job postings, the only place on CareerDeck where users write something
other people read.
**Written for:** the people who comment here, and the moderators who enforce this.

The short version is shown in the app before your first comment
([constants/policy.ts](../constants/policy.ts)). This is the long version, and the two say the same
things — if they ever disagree, this one is wrong and should be fixed.

---

## Who you are here

Comments are **anonymous to other people and tied to your account for us.**

A comment shows a generated name like `quiet-otter-4821`, and a badge composed from the school you
verified — `CS @ Purdue '27` — or simply "Verified" if you verified with a government ID instead.
It never shows your real name, your email address, or which school you *typed* into your profile
without confirming.

Behind that, every comment is attached to an account that cost you either a school email address or
an identity check to obtain. Nobody using the app can see that link. We can.

That asymmetry is the whole design, and it is worth being direct about why it exists: it is what
makes it safe for you to say that a named company treated you badly, and it is what makes it
expensive for somebody to harass you. If comments were fully anonymous, only the second half would
be true.

---

## What this is for

**Say what actually happened to you.** Ghosted after a final round. A recruiter who said one thing
and did another. An assessment that took four hours for a role that pays an hour's wage. An
interview that felt discriminatory. Name the company. Be specific about what happened and when.

This is the point of the product. Students find out what a hiring process is really like from other
students, and there is no other honest source for that. A comment section that flinches away from it
is a comment section that is not worth having.

**You will not be removed for being harsh about a company, or for swearing about one.** Criticism of
an employer, a recruiter's conduct in their professional role, a process, a pay band, or a decision
is allowed even when it is angry, even when it is unproven, and even when the company would rather
it were not there.

Two honest caveats about that:

- Write what happened, not what you assume. "They rejected me because I am international" is a
  claim about someone's motives; "they rejected me after asking about my visa status" is what you
  observed. The second one is more useful to the next person and far harder to argue with.
- A company can contact us about a specific comment. When they do, a human reads the comment against
  this policy — not against how much the company dislikes it. We will tell you if something of yours
  is removed, and why.

---

## What is not allowed

**Going after a person.** Harassment, abuse, or pile-ons aimed at an individual — another commenter,
a named recruiter, an interviewer, anybody. The line is not about tone, it is about target: you can
say a process was humiliating, you cannot say a person is worthless.

**Threats, and telling someone to harm themselves.** This is the one category where a first offence
can go straight to a ban rather than a warning.

**Sexual content**, and sexual remarks about anyone.

**Exposing anybody, including yourself.** No email addresses, phone numbers, street addresses,
government ID numbers, or payment details. No identifying an anonymous commenter. No "DM me on
Instagram" — if it cannot be said in the thread, moderation cannot see it, and that is precisely the
conversation that goes wrong.

The rule covers *your own* contact details too, which people find surprising. Recruiters read these
threads. Anything posted here is public and stays public.

**Spam.** Selling, recruiting, referral farming, link drops, or the same comment across many
postings.

**Making things up about a company.** Not "I disagree with the other comments" — that is a
disagreement, and disagreements are fine. This is about stating as fact something that did not
happen. It is the one restriction here that protects a company rather than a person, and it exists
because a fabricated claim about a named employer damages both them and the credibility of every
true comment next to it.

---

## How this is enforced

**Automatically, before a comment posts.** Contact details are matched by pattern and refused
outright. The rest of the text goes to a moderation classifier. It can refuse a comment, in which
case you are told what to change and your text is still in the box; or it can flag one, in which case
the comment posts normally and a moderator reads it. Flagging is not a punishment and you are not
told about it, because it usually means the classifier was unsure rather than that you did anything.

**By people, when something is reported.** Every report reaches a human. We aim to read reports
within 24 hours and anything involving a threat within one hour.

**Escalation is fixed and it is per account:**

| | What happens |
|---|---|
| First | A warning, with the comment removed and the reason sent to you |
| Second | You cannot comment for 7 days. You can still read, save, apply and track |
| Third | You cannot comment again |

A ban is tied to the school email address or the identity you verified with, not to the account.
Signing up again with a new email does not return commenting — you would need a different school
address or a different identity, which is the point.

**Rate limits**, separately from all of the above: 10 comments an hour and 40 a day. This is not a
judgement about you; it is what stops one account burying a thread.

## If we get it wrong

We will. The classifier will refuse something it should not have, and a moderator will remove
something that was fair.

- A refused comment tells you which rule it tripped. Rephrase and post it — that is not a
  workaround, it is the intended use.
- A removal or a restriction arrives as a notification with a reason. If it was a mistake, reply to
  the contact address below with the date and roughly what you wrote.
- Your account, your applications, your saved postings and your tracker are never affected by a
  comment decision. Only commenting is.

---

## Notice and takedown

If you believe a comment about you or your organisation is defamatory, infringing, or otherwise
unlawful, write to **[abuse@careerdeck.example](mailto:abuse@careerdeck.example)** with:

1. A link to the comment, or the posting and enough of the text to identify it.
2. What is wrong with it, and why.
3. How to reach you.

We will acknowledge within one business day and tell you the outcome. We remove content that breaks
this policy or the law. We do not remove a student's account of their own experience because it is
unflattering, and we will say so plainly rather than quietly complying.

We do not disclose who wrote a comment in response to a takedown request. That requires valid legal
process.

## Changes

This document carries a version, and the version is recorded against your account when you accept
it. If it changes in substance, the version changes and you will be asked again — so what we hold is
not "this user agreed to a policy" but "this user was shown this text on this date."
